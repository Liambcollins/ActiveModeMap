# Simulation results — active learning for mode-shape-vs-frequency mapping

**Setup.** Stand-in two-pathway EB contact-resonance solver (Multi75E-G geometry,
interior contact + free overhang, tilt, tip height, cone compliance, distributed
electrostatic load). Each "measurement" = park laser at x, sweep 161 frequencies
(10–530 kHz), record complex spectra for both PPLN domain signs. Noise: 8%
(of quasistatic signal) additive floor + 2% multiplicative, 4 µm laser-spot blur.
Benchmark: 6 random ground truths × 6 strategies × 3 scenarios, 10 positions each
(first 3 positions are a fixed seed design; the strategy chooses positions 4–10).

## Headline results

**1. Physics-informed inference is the whole game.** With the EB model in the
loop, the full x–f map and both operating points are recovered from **3–4 laser
positions** — versus the ~13–25 positions per load used in the manual detection-
position sweeps of the manuscript (Figs. 3–4), a ~4–8× reduction. The
model-agnostic GP baseline stalls at ~6% map RMSE (2+ orders of magnitude worse)
and cannot produce D-NS/D-ESBS estimates at all.

**2. Accuracy vs scenario** (median over ground truths, at N = 10 positions):

| scenario | map RMSE | D-NS error | D-ESBS error |
|---|---|---|---|
| well-specified | 1e-4 | ~5 nm | ~5 nm |
| 4× noise | 1e-4 | ~10 nm | ~10 nm |
| geometry mismatch | 2e-3 | ~0.25 µm | ~0.6 µm |

Even with deliberately perturbed generating geometry (tip set-back ±3 µm, height
±2 µm, tilt ±2°, overhang width ±0.15, tip mass ±50% — unknown to the inference),
spot localization stays **well below the 4 µm laser-spot floor** and near the
1 µm target. Detector noise is almost irrelevant: the physics constrains the
map far more than the data noise limits it.

**3. Acquisition strategy is a second-order effect in simulation.** All
physics-driven strategies converge by N≈4. Under model mismatch, EIG and
spot-targeted acquisition give the best D-NS (~0.24 µm vs 0.35–0.47 µm for
random/equispaced) while integrated-variance gives the best global map. EIG
consistently chooses near-tip/overhang positions — independently confirming the
manuscript's finding that the near-tip region carries the operating-point
information. Strategy choice will matter more in the experiment, where model
error is larger and drift makes early stopping valuable.

**4. What made inference work (directly transferable to the experiment):**
- staged fitting (global log-amplitude scan → log-amp refine → complex polish);
  naive complex least-squares reliably lands in a broad-damping local minimum;
- SNR-aware noise model (additive floor + ~3% fractional term);
- both domain signs measured at every position (separates piezo from
  electrostatic pathways — same reason the experiment uses PPLN);
- laser-spot blur folded into the likelihood;
- warm-started posteriors with a reduced-χ² guard triggering global re-scan.

## Practical protocol suggested by simulation

1. Seed with 3 positions (mid-lever, ~0.7 L, near-tip).
2. Fit posterior; report D-NS/D-ESBS with 95% CI after every position.
3. Acquire next position by EIG (map + spots) or spot-targeted (spots only).
4. Stop when spot CIs < 1 µm (typically N = 4–6) or when reduced-χ²
   indicates model error dominates — then fall back to local sampling around
   the predicted spots.

## Structural mismatch study (EB ≠ reality, as in the paper)

Because the manuscript shows the EB model does **not** quantitatively reproduce
experiment/FEM near the tip, we added a *structural* mismatch test: data
generated by a "pseudo-FEM" model (`ebal/hifi.py`) containing physics outside
the inference family — rotational apex constraint (3D tip-compliance proxy),
cone-concentrated electrostatic load, frequency-dependent damping, detection
sensitivity ripple. No theta can fit this data (reduced χ² ≈ 12 — the guard
detects it), and the best-fit EB carries ~1 µm systematic spot bias.

Two-layer defense (`ebal/hybrid.py`), median over 5 ground truths × randomized
perturbation strengths:

| method | D-NS error @ N=10 | D-ESBS error @ N=10 |
|---|---|---|
| pure physics (EIG) | 0.29 µm (plateau — bias) | 0.97 µm (plateau — bias) |
| hybrid | **0.03–0.2 µm** (still ↓) | **0.06 µm** |

1. **Kennedy–O'Hagan GP discrepancy**: complex residual spectra vs the EB fit
   are interpolated over x by a GP whose prior variance grows toward the tip
   (where the paper localizes beam-model error). Corrected map = EB + δ(x, f).
2. **Model-free spot extraction**: the domain pair separates pathways without
   the model — P = (plus−minus)/2 (piezo), E = (plus+minus)/2 (electrostatic).
   Spots come from phase-rotated **signed zero crossings** of the corrected
   P (on resonance) and E (quasistatic) profiles; a signed crossing survives
   the 4 µm laser-spot blur, where an amplitude minimum collapses onto the
   boundary. The EB model then only *steers where to sample*; the spot values
   are effectively measured, not modeled.
3. Acquisition `hybrid`: EIG for 2 steps after seeding, then bracket the
   predicted spots — which is also where the GP correction needs data.

See `figures/structural_mismatch.png`. Practical reading: even when the beam
model is wrong in the ways the paper documents, the AL loop still needs only
**~6–8 positions** to localize both operating points to ≲0.1–0.3 µm — because
the model's job is reduced to interpolation guidance and the spots are
extracted from corrected data.

## Caveats / next steps

- Stand-in solver: swap in CR2Beam / eb_cr_afm (same interface) and re-run
  `run_benchmark.py` — expected to be drop-in.
- The pseudo-FEM mismatch is still synthetic; the real test is the experiment
  (and optionally COMSOL maps as an intermediate: fit EB to FEM output offline
  to *learn the typical discrepancy shape* and use it as the GP prior).
- Friction/trace-retrace: measure trace and retrace as separate channels and
  either average (symmetric part) or model the asymmetry as an extra
  discrepancy output.
- Experiment phase: replace `VirtualAFM.measure` with an AEcroscopy driver
  (identical signature), add drift anchor re-measurement and per-load
  warm-started posteriors (previous load's posterior as prior).
